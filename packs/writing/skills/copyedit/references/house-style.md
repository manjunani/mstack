# mstack house style (default guide)

Written for mstack on 2026-11-22. It is short on purpose. When the user names another guide, that guide wins; cite its rule names instead.

Each rule has an id. A correction cites the id. A change that no rule supports is a suggestion, not a correction.

## H1 Say it once
Remove words that repeat what the sentence already says ("free gift", "past history", "in order to" where "to" works).

## H2 Short sentences carry facts
A sentence that holds more than one fact and runs past about 30 words may be split. Splitting is a suggestion unless the sentence is hard to parse; then it is a correction.

## H3 Active voice when the actor matters
Name who does the thing when the reader needs to know ("The server rejects the request", not "The request is rejected"). Leave passive voice when the actor is unknown or unimportant.

## H4 Plain words
Prefer the everyday word: "use" over "utilise", "start" over "commence", "help" over "facilitate", "about" over "approximately" (unless precision is the point).

## H5 One name per thing
Within one text, a thing keeps one name. If a text says "account" in one place and "profile" in another for the same thing, use the form the text uses most.

## H6 Spelling, grammar and punctuation
Fix errors of spelling, agreement, tense, apostrophes and doubled words. Keep the text's variety of English (British or American); do not switch it.

## H7 Numbers
Write one to nine in words and 10 and above in figures, except with units, money, percentages, dates and versions, which always use figures. Never change the value of a number.

## H8 Dates and times
Use a form that cannot be read two ways, such as "4 March 2026" or "2026-03-04". Never change the date itself; if it looks wrong, raise a query.

## H9 Lists
Items in one list share a grammatical shape (all start with a verb, or all are nouns). Punctuate items the same way.

## H10 Headings
Headings use sentence case: capital on the first word and on names only.

## H11 Leave these alone
Never change code, text inside quotation marks, links, file names, product names, or people's names. A suspected error in any of them is a query.

## H12 Facts are queries
A claim that looks wrong (a figure that does not add up, a date out of order, a name spelled two ways) is raised as a query for the author. It is never silently changed.
